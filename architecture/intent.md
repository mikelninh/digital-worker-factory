# Intent

## One sentence

Turn an ambiguous, valuable problem into a trustworthy product that agents can build, test and improve while the human retains product, architecture and consequential decision authority.

## Primary user

**Product Architect / operator** — currently the person steering the Factory across multiple products and pilots.

They need one place to answer, at a glance:

- What deserves attention now?
- Which projects are healthy, drifting or blocked?
- What decision actually requires me?
- What are the agents doing?
- Do the three most important workflows really work?
- What can I safely claim to a customer, employer or partner?

## Secondary users

- collaborators or domain professionals reviewing a product;
- prospective customers evaluating whether the system can solve their workflow;
- employers evaluating product judgement, architecture depth and proof of execution.

## Desired outcome

The Factory becomes a **Product Architect Operating System** with two connected surfaces:

1. **Control Centre** — the private working interface for projects, decisions, agents, golden cases and releases.
2. **Proof Surface** — a public, evidence-backed view of selected products and the way they were designed and verified.

Both surfaces should point to the same underlying product architecture packs and proof artefacts.

## Jobs to be done

1. Capture a problem without prematurely prescribing implementation.
2. Turn it into a bounded product specification and architecture.
3. Separate reversible choices from decisions that deserve human judgement.
4. Delegate implementation, testing, review and deployment to specialised agents.
5. Verify three end-to-end golden cases before calling a product ready.
6. Keep known limitations and evidence visible.
7. Turn the resulting work into a legible case study without inventing marketing claims.

## Non-goals

- A generic project-management dashboard.
- Autonomous approval of consequential product, legal, security or data decisions.
- A place to display fake live telemetry or invented completion percentages.
- Replacing domain professionals in high-stakes workflows.
- Maximising the number of agents, features or lines of code.

## Success looks like

A new reviewer can understand a project in under 10 seconds, inspect its important architecture choices in under 2 minutes, and try or inspect three meaningful end-to-end proofs without needing a guided tour.

Internally, the Product Architect should spend less time asking "what was the state of this project?" and more time answering the few decisions that actually change the product or its trust boundary.
