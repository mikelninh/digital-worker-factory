# Ethical Revenue Model — Digital Worker Factory

Digital Worker Factory should make organisations more capable without making unsafe autonomy, surveillance or human displacement the revenue incentive.

## Core rule

> **Charge for verified useful work completed inside the agreed authority boundary.**

## Legitimate paid value

- fixed-price workflow discovery and implementation;
- transparent subscription/support for deployed worker packs;
- integration and connector maintenance;
- assurance/evaluation packages;
- successful bounded workflow volume;
- reliability/operations support and agreed SLAs;
- measured client-value improvement where the metric is agreed before the pilot.

## Runtime billing invariant

A **Capability Outcome Receipt** is the stable metering surface.

- `executed` + policy satisfied → may be billable;
- `blocked` → not billable;
- `shadowed` → not billable as production execution;
- `failed` → not billable as successful work.

A pilot/discovery fee can of course cover evaluation work contractually, but the product must not represent blocked/failed calls as successful usage.

## What we do not monetise

- hidden per-token markups that make the bill impossible to predict;
- charging more because the worker loops or fails repeatedly;
- selling customer communications, documents or behavioural data;
- broad employee surveillance unrelated to the contracted workflow;
- bypassing human approval to increase throughput;
- pretending a proposal/draft is a completed external action;
- making safety/eval evidence a premium feature customers must disable to save money.

## Human/workforce covenant

Automation should target repetitive administrative work and measurable service quality. Customer pilots should disclose:

- which workflow is being automated;
- which actions remain human-controlled;
- what data is processed;
- what success metric is being measured;
- what happens to exceptions/failures;
- whether the goal is capacity, quality, response time or cost reduction.

Do not claim a workforce outcome that has not been measured. Where automation changes roles materially, recommend a transition/reskilling plan rather than hiding the impact behind "efficiency" language.

## Preferred commercial sequence

### First customer

`fixed 7-day sprint → measured baseline → bounded implementation → human-supervised shadow/copilot → before/after report`

### Repeatable product

`subscription/support + successful bounded workflow units + integration/assurance`

The commercial north star remains **collected cash and measured client value**, but a value metric is invalid if it rewards privacy loss, unsafe autonomy or degraded service to the end customer.
